# whats-for-tea
What’s for tea? is a simple cooking app that answers the question everyone asks at the end of a long day. Instead of scrolling through endless recipe sites, you tell it how long you have, what you’re eating and what’s in your fridge, and it suggests three dinners you can actually make tonight.

Every recipe is written for beginners and built from ordinary supermarket ingredients. Each one shows exactly what to buy, scales to the number of people you’re cooking for, and walks you through the method in clear, numbered steps with times, heat levels and safe cooking checks. When you’re ready to cook, cook mode shows one step at a time in large, easy-to-read text, keeps your screen awake, and offers a built-in timer whenever a step mentions minutes, so you can read it from across the kitchen.

Planning ahead is just as easy. Plan my week picks five dinners for Monday to Friday based on your filters, and one tap adds every ingredient to your shopping list. The list combines duplicates, lets you tick items off as you shop, and can be copied and sent to someone else. Found a favourite? Save it and filter to see only the recipes you love.

The standout feature is Chef chat, an AI cooking assistant that creates original recipes for you. Tell it you have lentils and a tired courgette, that you need a cheap vegan meal in twenty minutes, or that you want a fun twist on pasta night, and it invents a dish with ingredients, steps and timings. Its recipes open in the same recipe view, so you can scale them, add them to your shopping list, save them and cook them with cook mode.

The app comes with nine simple classics, including garlic and chilli spaghetti, egg fried rice, chickpea curry and a one-tray chicken traybake, plus links to more ideas on BBC Good Food. It works on phones, tablets and computers, supports light and dark mode, and stores your lists and saved recipes privately on your own device.

What’s for tea? makes deciding, shopping and cooking simple again.
